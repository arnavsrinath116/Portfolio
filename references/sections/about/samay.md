## Small details worth copying
- **The tab bar is the navigation.** Real-looking pinned tabs on the left (Figma files, Claude, ChatGPT) are jokes that show how he works. The "samay" tab group holds the real sections. The active tab, the URL (`samay.me/about` → `/work-log` → `/journeys`…) and a row of **progress dashes** under the window all move with the scroll.
- **A "keep scrolling | next: work log ↓" pill** in the window's corner tells you what's next. At the end it turns into a loop back to the start.
- **Two voices:** clean sans and serif for the facts, **handwritten script** for the asides (*"yep, that's me"*, *"if you want to be formal"*). It reads like he's talking to you.
- **One idea per tab,** each with the same two-line heading formula: plain line + orange italic line.
- **Small personal facts as stickers** (*someday, k2*), not a skills list.
- The page keeps a **vertical scroll gauge** on the right edge (a pencil marking %) through the whole site.

## What I'd take for my site
1. **The structure:** one framed object, scrolled through in chapters. For me it could be a **drawing sheet or Fusion window** with tabs or layers instead of a browser, to stay in the blueprint world.
2. **Personal chapters,** not a CV: who I am · where I've worked or studied · my cameras and photography · a playlist or what I listen to while I work · a small gallery.
3. **The "next: … ↓" pill** and the progress dashes, so a long pinned section never feels endless.
4. **Handwritten asides** next to the clean type, in moderation.

## What I'd do differently
- It's long: 5 tabs of pinned scroll (~4,700 px). Keep mine to 3–4 chapters, and let people **click the tabs** to jump.
- The loader in front of the site is a heavy orange screen. Not needed.
